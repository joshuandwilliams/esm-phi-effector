## Purpose

This document reviews every keyword used to map free-text PHI-base `Function` annotations to broad biochemical classes for fine-tuning ESM-2 with LoRA. Mislabelled training data degrades model performance, so each keyword is evaluated for correctness and assigned a confidence level.

**Confidence levels:**
- 🟢 **HIGH** — keyword clearly belongs in this category based on established biochemistry
- 🟡 **MEDIUM** — keyword is reasonable but has edge cases or ambiguity
- 🟠 **LOW** — keyword is likely misclassified or too broad, and may introduce label noise
---
## 1. Pre-filter: Putative Recoverable

| Keyword | Verdict | Confidence |
|---------|---------|------------|
| `putative transcription factor` | ✅ Keep in TF/Regulator | 🟢 HIGH |
| `putative transcriptional regulator` | ✅ Keep in TF/Regulator | 🟢 HIGH |
**Rationale:** "Putative" in PHI-base often just means the gene has not been experimentally characterised in that specific organism, but the domain architecture is clear. Zn2Cys6 domains, bZIP domains, and helix-turn-helix motifs are well-conserved, so putative TFs are almost always genuine TFs. Recovering these before the `putative` filter is correct.

**Rationale:** These entries lack functional annotation. Including them would introduce noise. The ordering — recover specific putative TFs first, then drop all remaining putative/hypothetical — is correct.

Exochitinases have well-characterised catalytic domains (GH18/GH19 families), so this is a borderline loss. However, for training data purity, dropping is safer than adding every putative-prefixed entry to the recoverable list.

## 12. Recommended New Category: Redox / Oxidative Stress

Several misplaced keywords from Secondary metabolite / Biosynthesis, plus uncaptured entries, form a coherent group:

- NADPH oxidase (18)
- catalase (11) + catalase-peroxidase (12)
- superoxide dismutase (16)  

These proteins all participate in reactive oxygen species (ROS) metabolism. In plant-pathogen interactions, ROS production (oxidative burst) and detoxification are critical for both host defence and pathogen virulence (Heller & Tudzynski, 2011, *Annu Rev Phytopathol*). These enzymes share evolutionary and structural relationships (peroxidase-catalase superfamily, SOD metalloenzyme families).  

**However**, this class would be small (~57 sequences). For ESM-2 fine-tuning with limited classes, you may prefer to:

1. Fold them into Kinase / Signalling (since ROS acts as a signalling molecule), or
2. Drop them entirely rather than create an undersized class, or
3. Accept the small class if balance isn't critical.

## 14. A Note on ESM-2 Classification Implications

For fine-tuning ESM-2 with LoRA, label purity matters more than label coverage. It is better to drop ambiguous sequences (return None) than to assign them an incorrect class, because:
1. **ESM-2 embeddings capture structural/evolutionary features.** A beta-lactamase in the "Protease" class will have embeddings far from true proteases, confusing the classifier.
2. **Label noise disproportionately hurts small classes.** If your Protease class has 300 sequences and 15 are beta-lactamases, that's 5% noise — enough to degrade decision boundaries.
3. **Broad keywords like `dehydrogenase` can introduce >50 mislabelled sequences** across multiple classes, which is a significant source of systematic error.
The conservative approach — drop what you can't confidently classify — will yield a cleaner training signal even if the dataset is somewhat smaller.
